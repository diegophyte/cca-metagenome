### FastQC
- the first module gives basic stats for the sample 
	- generally its a good idea to keep track of the total number of reads sequenced for each sample and to make sure the read length and %GC content is as expected
#### per base sequence quality: 
- plot provides the distribution of quality scores at each position in the read across all reads. can alert us to whether there were any problems occuring during sequncing 
- y-axis = quality scores 
- x-axis = position in the read 
- the quality scores on the given example appear to drop going from the beginning toward the end of the reads. --> not unexpected for reads generated by illumina sequencing
- sequencing error profiles:
	- for illumina- the quality of the nucleotide base calls are related to the signal intensity and purity of the fluorescent signal. low intensity fluorescence or the presence of multiple different fluorescent signals can lead to a drop in the quality score assigned to the nucleotide 
	- signal decay: as sequencing proceeds the fluorescent signal intensity decays with each cycle which causes a decrease in quality scores at the 3' end of the read 
	- phasing: as the number of cycles increases the signal starts to blur as the cluster loses synchronicity. as the cycle progresses, some strands get random failure of nucleotides to incorporate due to:
		- Incomplete removal of the 3’ terminators and fluorophores
		- Incorporation of nucleotides without effective 3’ terminators
	- overclustering: sequencing facilities can overcluster the flow cells which results in small distance between clusters and an overlap in the signals. the two clusters can be interpreted as a single cluster with mixed fluorescent signals being detected, decreasing signal purity, generating lower quality scores across the entire read.   
#### per sequence quality scores 
- gives the average quality score on teh x axis and the number of sequences with that average on the y axis. --> ideally want majority of reads to have a high average quality score with no large bumps at the lower quality values
#### per base sequence content 
- always gives a FAIL for RNA-seq data - first 10-12 bases result from the random hexamer priming that occurs during RNA-seq library prep
- also shows whether the proportions of A,T,G,C remain relatively flat and consistent across the read length.
- substantial deviation or a major difference between A-T or G-C pairs at any position can indicate adapter contamination, overrepresented sequences or biased fragmetation.
#### per sequence GC content 
- plot gives the GC distribution over all sequences. generally is a good idea to note whetehr the GC content of the central peak corresponds to the expected %GC for the organism. 
- distribution should be normal unless over represented sequences or contamnination with another organism

#### sequence duplication levels
- plot can help identify a low complexity library whic could stem from: 
	- too many cycles of PCR 
	- too little starting material 
- for rna seq we don't normally do anything to address this in the analysis, but if this were a pilot experiment, we might adjust the number of PCR cycles, amount of input, or amount of sequencing for future libraries
#### overrepresented sequences
- displays the sequences that occur in more than 0.1% of the total number of sequences. 
- aids in identifying contamination 
- if the %GC was off above, this table can help identify the source
